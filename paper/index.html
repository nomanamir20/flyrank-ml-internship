Prioritising Keyword-Article Content Associated With Observed Performance Decline

A machine-learning decision-support study using anonymized FlyRank internship data to identify content items that resemble patterns associated with observed decline and to prioritise them for human review.

Abstract

Five-sentence summary

This study asks whether machine-learning signals can help prioritise keyword-article content associated with observed performance decline. The analysis uses an anonymized FlyRank dataset, filters the selected keyword-article lane, defines a binary declining-content label, and evaluates a model against a simple baseline using client-grouped validation. The modelling pipeline excludes identifiers and observed trend variables that could introduce leakage and treats the resulting score as a prioritisation signal rather than causal evidence. The resulting model and baseline are compared on the same validation design, with the model's predictions converted into a ranked review queue. The final output is therefore a human-review prioritisation system: it identifies content that resembles previously observed decline patterns, while making no guarantee that a recommended intervention will improve future SEO performance.

01 · Introduction

Problem Statement

Content teams often have more pages to review than they can investigate manually. When search visibility or engagement declines, the challenge is not simply detecting that decline has occurred; it is deciding which content should receive attention first.

This research explores a machine-learning approach to that prioritisation problem. Instead of automatically modifying content, the system produces a ranked queue that can help a human reviewer decide where investigation may be most valuable.

Research question: Can observable content, search, engagement, freshness, and related performance features be used to prioritise keyword-article items that resemble patterns associated with observed decline?

The distinction between prediction and explanation is central to this work. A model score indicates similarity to patterns learned from the available data. It does not establish that a particular feature caused the decline.

03 · Methodology

Methodology

Assumptions

  1. The task is treated as binary classification and ranking.
  2. The model is used for decision-support and human review, not automatic content changes.
  3. Rows represent keyword-article content items in the selected analysis lane.
  4. Client IDs are used for grouped validation only and are not model features.
  5. Content IDs are identifiers only and are not model features.
  6. The observed label is treated as an evaluation outcome, not as a causal explanation.

Label definition

Target variable: is_declining_label

Target type: Binary classification

Positive class: 1 = declining

Negative class: 0 = not declining

Feature definition

Forty model features are used after excluding the target, trend-derived variables, and identifiers. The feature inventory consists of 29 numeric and 11 categorical variables.

Leakage controls

  • trend_direction excluded.
  • trend_pct excluded.
  • is_declining_label excluded.
  • client_id excluded from model features.
  • content_id excluded from model features.

Validation design

Client-grouped validation is used so that validation clients do not overlap with training clients. This design is intended to reduce the risk that the model benefits from client-specific information appearing in both training and validation groups.

Model use

The resulting model score is used as a prioritisation signal for human review. It is not treated as causal evidence and does not automatically trigger content changes.

Ranking performance

The model achieved a Precision@20, Precision@50, and Precision@100 of 1.0000. This means that all evaluated top-20, top-50, and top-100 positions in the generated ranking corresponded to the positive class under the notebook's evaluation definition.

The model achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.8764 and an Average Precision of 0.9034. These values indicate that the model provides meaningful separation and ranking performance within the evaluated data and validation design.

Comparison with baseline

The baseline achieved the same perfect Precision@20, Precision@50, and Precision@100 values as the model. However, the baseline also produced a ROC-AUC of 1.0000 and an Average Precision of 1.0000, both higher than the corresponding model values of 0.8764 and 0.9034.

Result interpretation: Based on the executed Section 4 metrics, the model does not outperform the baseline on ROC-AUC or Average Precision. Both approaches achieve perfect Precision@20, Precision@50, and Precision@100 under the reported evaluation. Therefore, the results should be reported descriptively rather than claiming that the machine-learning model improves upon the baseline.

Why the results matter

The evaluation demonstrates that the model can produce a highly concentrated ranking of observed declining items at the evaluated top-k positions. At the same time, the comparison shows that strong top-k precision does not necessarily imply superior overall ranking performance. ROC-AUC and Average Precision provide additional information about how well scores separate and rank the two classes across the evaluated population.

05 · Ranked Recommendations

Action Playbook

The model output is converted into a ranked review queue. The goal is to help a human reviewer focus attention on higher-priority content rather than treating every content item equally.

1 Prioritise high-decline-score content for review

Begin with content receiving the highest model scores because these items most strongly resemble the patterns associated with the observed declining class in the available modelling data.

2 Investigate recent performance and freshness signals

Review recent impressions, clicks, sessions, content age, freshness, and related performance signals to understand the context surrounding each prioritised item.

3 Use model recommendations as a review queue, not an automatic action

A high score should trigger investigation rather than an automatic rewrite, deletion, or SEO intervention.

4 Validate the recommendation against the content itself

Human reviewers should inspect the actual page, its search intent, freshness, usefulness, and other relevant context before deciding whether an intervention is appropriate.

5 Monitor outcomes after interventions

Where a content intervention is made, its future performance should be monitored rather than assuming that the model's recommendation itself guarantees improvement.

Recommended operating principle: Use the model to decide what to investigate first, not what to change automatically.

07 · Limitations

Limitations & Honest Framing

1. Observational data

The analysis uses observed search and content-performance data. Associations identified by the model should not be interpreted as causal relationships.

2. Prediction is not explanation

A high decline score indicates that an item resembles patterns associated with declining content in the training data. It does not explain why that content declined.

3. Human review is required

The model is intended as a prioritisation and decision-support signal. Recommendations should be reviewed by a human before any content or SEO action is taken.

4. Generalisation

Client-grouped validation reduces the risk of client overlap between training and validation data, but performance on completely different organisations, markets, or future data may differ.

5. Missing data

Several input variables contain missing values. The modelling pipeline therefore depends on its handling of missing information, and predictions may be less reliable when key signals are unavailable.

6. Label limitations

The declining label represents an observed outcome defined from the available dataset. It should not be treated as a complete measure of content quality, SEO quality, or business value.

7. No guaranteed improvement

A recommended action is a prioritisation suggestion, not a guarantee that updating content will improve rankings, traffic, engagement, or conversions.

8. Public-safety framing

The published research artifact should use aggregated results and public-safe descriptions. Client identifiers, private queries, private URLs, and other sensitive information should not be exposed.

Honest claim: The model identifies and prioritises content items that resemble patterns associated with observed decline. It does not prove causation, guarantee future performance, or guarantee that a recommended intervention will improve SEO outcomes.
