Section 1

Abstract

This study investigates whether machine learning can help prioritize keyword-article content that shows signals of decline so that human reviewers can focus their attention on the highest-priority cases.

Using the anonymized FlyRank internship dataset, the analysis frames decline detection as a binary classification and ranking problem and evaluates a learned model using client-grouped validation to reduce the risk of client-specific leakage.

On the held-out validation set of 2,789 rows from 5 clients, the validated model achieved Precision@20 of 0.9500, Precision@50 of 0.9600, Precision@100 of 0.9600, ROC-AUC of 0.6696, and Average Precision of 0.8568.

The resulting scores are used to create a ranked review queue and action playbook rather than to automatically rewrite, delete, redirect, or publish content.

The results support the use of the model as a decision-support signal for human review, while the model's limitations, validation scope, and non-causal interpretation require contextual inspection before any content decision is made.
